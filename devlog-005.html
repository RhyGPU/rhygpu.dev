Devlog 005: Fighting "Prose Gravity" with a Hierarchy of Truth

June 2026

In the previous logs, I documented the birth of the Soul and the World Log—the dual-system architecture meant to kill AI amnesia [1, 2]. The hidden state loop was finally "breathing," and the engine was managing character data outside the main prompt [3]. But as soon as I moved into intensive roleplay testing, I hit a wall that every AI developer eventually faces.

I call it Prose Gravity.

The Catalyst: The "Live Grenade" Incident

The setup was simple: Aurora (the test character) reacted to a Tinder phone screen, took the device, locked it, and tossed it onto the couch before moving to the kitchen [4].

In the very next turn, despite the history being right there in the context, the model described her as "still holding their phone like it's a live grenade" [4, 5].

The Realization: Recent chat is just a transcript to an LLM; it is not a binding law [5]. A long, emotional 800-word paragraph about a phone reveal carries more "attention weight" than a single sentence at the end of the message stating the phone was put away [6].

The Pivot: From Transcript to State

The AI's "Narrator" was fighting with the "Engine's" reality. My initial reaction was to try and track every minor detail—the location of every fork and chair—but I quickly realized that was "too much shit" [7]. It would bloat the state and fight the project’s core goal: less tokens, best outcome [8].

The solution wasn't better memory; it was a Hierarchy of Truth. I needed to tell the AI exactly which parts of the prompt were authoritative and which were just "flavor" [6].

The Implementation: The Context Priority Stack

I refactored context_compiler.rs to implement a ranked Context Priority Stack [9, 10]. Instead of just dumping history into the prompt, the engine now compiles a "State Brief" where priority is enforced by placement and explicit labeling:

  • P0: Latest User Input — The immediate new action that overrides everything else [11].
  • P1: [SCENE STATE] — The authoritative physical facts: what is true now [9, 12].
  • P2: [DO NOT REPLAY] — Explicit instructions marking beats that are completed and must not be resurrected [12, 13].
  • P3: [CHARACTER/WORLD SNAPSHOT] — The underlying psyche and environment [9, 12].
  • P4: [RECENT CHAT] — Lower-priority excerpts used for tone and dialogue flow only [11, 12].

The "Tail-End" Breakthrough

While debugging the Priority Stack using the LLM Payload Inspector, I found a massive technical flaw: the engine was accidentally feeding the model the beginning of long assistant responses instead of the end [14, 15].

In a 1,200-character response, the "setup" (Aurora seeing the phone) happens at the start, but the "final state" (Aurora tossing the phone) happens at the end. By feeding the head of the message, I was literally forcing the AI to re-anchor to a state that no longer existed [14].

I implemented a Tail-based excerpt system [16, 17]:

  1. Strip the Noise: The engine strips status blocks, hidden state JSON, and sensory "clutter" before processing [16].
  2. Grab the Tail: It takes only the last 700–1,200 characters of the response, ensuring the most recent physical actions are what the LLM reads first [16, 18].

The Result

By reordering the feed so the Latest Exchange appeared at the bottom of the system context—closest to the new user message—I successfully broke the Prose Gravity [19, 20]. Aurora finally stopped "resurrecting" the phone and started staying in the kitchen where the state engine said she was [21].

The takeaway: Continuity isn't just about memory; it’s about enforcing a hierarchy of reality. The Narrator can be creative, but the Engine must be the law.
