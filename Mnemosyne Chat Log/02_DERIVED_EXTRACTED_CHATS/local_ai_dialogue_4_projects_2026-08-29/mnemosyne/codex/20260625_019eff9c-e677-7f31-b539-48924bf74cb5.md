## 0915. ASSISTANT | 2026-07-24T01:07:47.158Z | final_answer

It **can absolutely be bad to add settings indiscriminately**. Every setting becomes another behavior to explain, test, persist, migrate, and keep compatible across providers.

For Mnemosyne, the better rule is:

- Add a setting when RP users repeatedly need control over the outcome.
- Hide specialist controls under Advanced.
- Show provider-specific controls only when supported.
- Offer strong presets before exposing individual parameters.
- Keep diagnostics and engine-contract controls in Dev Mode.
- Avoid settings that merely expose internal implementation details.

SillyTavern’s identity is maximum configurability. Mnemosyne’s identity should be **persistent roleplay state with unusually good defaults**, while still allowing depth when somebody goes looking for it.

Good future additions include streaming behavior, stop sequences, context budget, continuation handling, auto-scroll, message editing confirmations, composer behavior, accessibility, prompt-format presets, and character-specific generation profiles. But adding every sampler or obscure compatibility switch would make Mnemosyne harder to understand without making the roleplay meaningfully better.

So yes to more settings, but through **progressive disclosure and demonstrated user value**, not raw quantity.
